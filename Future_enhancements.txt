The better design
Reframe the task. For your use case it isn't even open-set classification — it's adversarial verification: given a declared CTH and description, find the strongest legally-supported alternative and compute the duty differential. That's a better-posed, easier, and more valuable problem than 12,000-way prediction, and it matches how you actually work.
Stage 0 — Enrichment, not normalization. BE descriptions are deliberately impoverished. Resolve part numbers against datasheets where possible; attach importer profile (a logistics company importing Amikacin Sulphate is a feature, not noise); attach BE-level context — all sibling lines, supplier, FTA claim, notification claimed. The Transtree pattern is undetectable at line level and trivial at importer level.
Stage 1 — Recall-oriented candidate generation, deliberately cross-chapter. Hybrid retrieval (BM25 + dense) over three indexes: heading/subheading terms from the current First Schedule; an alias/exemplar index built from ruling descriptions; and a precedent index of contested pairs. Output 10–15 candidate headings spanning chapters. Embeddings live here, where their failure mode (over-recall) is harmless. Never commit top-down.
Stage 2 — Deterministic legal constraint pass. For each candidate, mechanically pull the governing Section Notes, Chapter Notes, supplementary notes, and — critically — every exclusion note anywhere in the schedule that references that heading. Many Note 1 exclusion lists are encodable as hard rules. This prunes candidates and produces the legal docket for stage 3. Note retrieval is keyed by the tariff's own cross-reference structure, not similarity.
Stage 3 — LLM as GIR adjudicator with tools, not validator. The LLM walks the sequence explicitly: GIR 1 against heading terms + docket notes → 2(a) incomplete/unassembled (with sibling lines in context) → 3(a) specificity → 3(b) essential character → 3(c) → GIR 6 at subheading, with the 8-digit text verified against the actual schedule (your own canonical rule — 90189031 taught you that sub-headings lie). Output is a reasoned memo with pinpoint citations to note text, not a score. This is what the agentic-workflow paper actually proposes.
Stage 4 — Precedent check. Retrieve CAAR/CESTAT/SC rulings on the surviving contested pair specifically. A 9032-vs-8537 call with a CAAR ruling on point is a finding; without one it's a Manual Verification Queue entry. This maps directly onto your existing workbook structure.
Stage 5 — Calibrated routing by stakes, not softmax. Conformal prediction sets ("the answer is in {8537.10, 9032.89} at 90%") instead of point confidence, and route human review by revenue at stake — the BCD/IGST differential between declared and best-alternative, run through your AIFTA-adjusted revenue test. A confidently-detected revenue-neutral reclassification should never reach your queue; an ambiguous ₹40L differential always should.
Training/gold data: AAR and CAAR rulings are your goldmine — labeled examples with expert rationales, exactly the supervision a GIR-reasoning model needs. Supplement with CESTAT decisions, WCO classification opinions, EU BTI and US CROSS at the harmonized 6-digit level, and your own confirmed audit findings as a private adversarial test set. Declared BE data is used only as a weak signal for the alias index and as the input distribution — never as labels.
Where ML actually earns its keep here: dense retrieval recall in stage 1, attribute extraction from filthy descriptions (material, function, completeness, assembly state), and precedent matching in stage 4. The classification decision itself is rules + reasoning + precedent + human, because that's what survives a CESTAT challenge — and "the embedding said so" does not appear in any speaking order ever written.
One closing jab at the original: it ends with "return top-3 codes with confidence scores so an expert can decide." That's the system admitting it can't do the job and outsourcing the hard part to you — while having stripped away the legal docket you'd need to decide. The redesign inverts it: the system does the legal legwork (notes, exclusions, precedents, duty math) and you do the judgment. That division of labor is the only one that works in this domain.
